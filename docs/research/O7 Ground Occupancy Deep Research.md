## Interpretation for the downstream ruling

1. **The cavalry linear bound is doctrinally firm and independent of Little Bighorn.** A dismounted US cavalry skirmish line in the 1870s occupies roughly **2.5–2.75 m of front per firing man at prescribed interval**, compressible to ~0.9 m/man when reinforced, with the important structural caveat that **one man in four holds horses and is off the line** — so a 48-man company fields ~36 firers across its front. Feature capacity and unit extent for regular cavalry should be built on the interval-plus-horse-holder model, not on a packed-line assumption.
2. **Mounted and dismounted warrior regimes must stay separate, and neither yields a clean dispersed spacing.** The only firm mounted figure is a *minimum footprint* (~2.4 m² per horse); dispersed mounted spacing is UNRESOLVED. Dismounted massed-under-cover density is governed by terrain, not interval — model it as cover-feature occupancy (warriors fill available ravines/pits/rocks) rather than as men-per-meter.
3. **Area-per-man is a confirmed gap, not an oversight.** Drill literature supplies only a nominal ~0.17 m² close-order body footprint; there is no publication-grade area-per-man for a fighting body in either culture. Any downstream area figure must be flagged as a modeling assumption, not a sourced datum.
