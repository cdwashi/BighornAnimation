## §3 — The boundary question — RULED at adjudication as proposed

The adjudicator's pre-ruling extends the Libby boundary to GNIS: "a GNIS record
fetched from a web query is a relay, not a page — referent-capable, tier-incapable."
**This registration asks for that to be argued rather than inherited, because the
analogy fails at its load-bearing step and the collision is with D112's own ruled
text.** The Libby boundary's rationale: a fetched TRANSCRIPTION of a book can differ
from the page — the page is the physical artifact, and Chuck's stage-2 read reaches
it. GNIS has no page behind the record: **it is a federal database whose canonical,
authoritative form IS the queried record at USGS's own endpoint.** A human visiting
the GNIS search site performs the same fetch. If every fetch of GNIS is a relay,
D112's ruled grant condition — "the GNIS record gets opened and recorded per claim,
then the upgrade is granted" — is UNSATISFIABLE BY ANY MEANS, which cannot be what
D112 ruled. **Proposed rule, for adjudication:** a record retrieved from the
AUTHORITATIVE ENDPOINT (USGS GNIS at its .gov service), with feature name, feature
ID, coordinates, and query date recorded, IS the page-level read of that source;
third-party mirrors (TopoZone, HometownLocator — both already named in the Crow's
Nest file) remain relays under the Libby boundary. If adjudication instead holds
the line, the item still runs — but its grant condition needs a dated amendment
naming what WOULD satisfy it, so a ruled condition does not stand unsatisfiable in
the record.
