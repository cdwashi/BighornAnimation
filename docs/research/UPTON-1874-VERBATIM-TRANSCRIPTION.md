## The habitual interval — ¶610, p. 243 (eye-verified)

> **610.** The habitual interval between skirmishers is five yards. When the captain wishes to deploy with a greater or less interval than five yards, he states the intervals between the skirmishers and between the fours, and then gives the commands for deployment as before.

This paragraph sits in the mounted skirmishers school; ¶656 (p. 256, *[text layer]*) extends it to dismounted work: "The skirmishers dismounted execute all the movements explained for skirmishers when mounted, by the same commands and means," with a stand-fast modification for the designated skirmisher.

## Dismounted deploy forward — ¶652, p. 255 (eye-verified)

> **652.** *To deploy forward.* The captain commands: 1. *As skirmishers,* 2. *On left (right or centre) four take intervals,* 3. MARCH.
>
> At the command *march,* the left four moves straight to the front; the other fours make a partial wheel to the right, and the movement is completed as when mounted, **except that the interval between fours is fifteen yards,** and the deployment is made on number three of each four, who stands fast; **numbers two and one move respectively five and ten yards to his right.**

Number four of each four is the horse-holder and is not on the line (established throughout the dismounted school; cf. ¶648–649, p. 253 *[text layer]*: all "except number four of each four, the guard for the led horses… dismount").

## To dismount skirmishers to fight on foot — ¶665, p. 257 (*[text layer]*)

> **665.** The captain sounds the rally by platoon, which being executed, he causes each chief to dismount his platoon and to deploy it. The led horses are conducted to the company reserve.

¶665 prescribes the *procedure*, not the interval; the interval figures live in ¶610 and ¶652 above — which is why the O7 researcher, working from the index, could not settle the number from this paragraph's entry alone.

## What this settles, and what it corrects

1. **Upton's own habitual skirmish interval is five yards (4.57 m), not three.** The O7 report's Q1 *best* bound (~2.74 m, "three yards") was inferred from the volume's index terms; the text layer shows every "three yards" index entry is the **horse interval** of the School of the Trooper (pp. 138, 145, 167: "intervals of three yards are taken between the horses"; "each horse with his rider occupies a space of three yards in length by one in breadth") — a misattributed index term, not a skirmish spacing. Cooke 1862's two-to-three paces (1.52–2.29 m) stands as the earlier doctrine's figure.
2. **The dismounted deploy-forward line is clustered, not uniform:** three firers per four at five-yard intervals (a 10-yard span), fifteen yards between fours. Linear front per firing man depends on how "interval between fours" is read:
   - **uniform reading** (15 yd measured no.3-to-no.3, adjacent fours' end men 5 yd apart): ~5 yd = **4.57 m per firing man**;
   - **gap reading** (15 yd of clear space between fours, the drill-usage sense of "interval"): 25-yd period per 3 firers ≈ 8⅓ yd = **7.62 m per firing man**.
   Both readings are recorded; the choice is a ruling, not a research finding.
3. **The doctrinal envelope for an 1876 dismounted cavalry skirmish line is therefore 4.57–7.62 m of front per firing man (Upton 1874), with 1.52–2.29 m (Cooke 1862) as the prior doctrine's tighter interval and ~0.91 m (Carpenter) as the densest field practice.** At ~112 firers, Upton's habitual gives ~512 m of front — O5's B5 derivation from RCOI testimony ("five to 10 yards apart") is **rehabilitated**: the troopers testified to spacing that matches the current manual. The encoded 206 m (~1.84 m/man) sits **below Upton doctrine entirely**, inside Cooke 1862's band.
4. **Correction chain, all dated in the register:** O5 (secondary; "~5-yard standard" → ~500 yd, called 206 m contradicted) → O7 (index terms; "5 yd is the high bound, 3 yd best; 206 m inside the envelope") → **this transcription (printed page; 5 yd is the habitual standard; the 3-yd best was a horse-interval misattribution; 206 m below Upton doctrine)**.
