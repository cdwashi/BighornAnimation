# O5-RESOURCE — Citation Verification & Upgrade: Battle of the Little Bighorn Simulation (25 June 1876)

## TL;DR
- **The single largest evidentiary gap is confirmed:** no publication-grade cartridge-distribution map of Reno's valley skirmish line exists in the open literature. Jason Pitsch's private valley finds were plotted in a publication-grade NPS report (Scott 2010, Technical Report 124, Fig. 26) only as scattered find-spots, not as a firing-pin-matched skirmish-line frontage map; the fuller collection is privately held on Pitsch's own land.
- **Several load-bearing figures rest only on tier-5 material:** the **"225-yard" skirmish line** (no publication-grade source gives that number, and a spacing derivation contradicts it — CONFIRMED-WEAK), the **"more than 500 warriors" against Reno** (a modern gloss, not verbatim in Schultz's 1926 Jackson memoir — CONFIRMED-WEAK/RELAYED), and the **pony-herd high bound of 20,000** (journalism, not Gray/Michno — CONFIRMED-WEAK). The encoded pony-herd low/best and high **measure different quantities** (dawn-visible subset vs. total herd) — a definition error, not a spread.
- **Where scholarship is strong, claims upgrade cleanly:** the **Cooke note wording** (verbatim in the 1879 RCOI transcript), the **100-carbine/24-revolver ammunition load**, the **~210 Custer-wing dead**, the **five-company annihilation / seven-company siege endstate**, and the **Springfield extraction-failure rate (~3%)** all have publication-grade support.

## Key Findings

**CONTRADICTED / definitional-error findings (highest-value output — lead with these):**

- **B1 pony-herd measurement mismatch — CONFIRMED.** The prior hand-audit is correct: the Bigelow/INSCOM "2,000–3,000" figure and the NPS/popular "~15,000–25,000" figure **measure different quantities.** The low figure describes the herd *visible from the Crow's Nest at dawn* (a near-village subset) and is further contaminated by conflation with the *warrior* count of "2,000–3,000"; the higher figures estimate the *total* herd. The encoded low/best (2,000/2,500) and high (20,000) are therefore not a coherent single estimate. This is a measurement-definition error, not a legitimate low/best/high spread. No verdict of CONTRADICTED is assigned only because no single scholarly source asserts the encoded triad to be contradicted — but the parameter as encoded is internally incoherent and must be split.

- **A-CASUALTIES coalition figure — the encoded "low 31 (HistoryNet)" is not the lowest cited figure, and "high 300" is explicitly the discredited bound.** Per Wikipedia (citing Native American listings by name): "Estimates of Native American casualties have differed widely, from as few as 36 dead (from Native American listings of the dead by name) to as many as 300." The "31 named warriors" figure is a modern name-reconciliation (Wild West / HistoryNet); Red Horse's 1877 testimony gave the high figure. These must be reported as a spread with a source per bound, never averaged.

**Strong, clean upgrades:** Cooke note wording; ammunition loads; company organization/battalion strengths; ~210 Custer-wing dead and 268 total U.S. dead; the five-company-destroyed / seven-company-siege endstate; Springfield extraction archaeology; the Medicine Tail "charge vs. feint" DISPUTED label.

**Persistent page-level gaps:** Gray's exact watch-time offset in minutes (B4) and Michno's "Bench" at p. 105 (B3) could not be verified against the primary text and are reported RELAYED / UNRESOLVED. Neither Gray 1991 nor Michno 1997 is available in readable full-text online (Michno on Internet Archive is lending-restricted).

Source per bound:
- **Dawn-visible subset (~2,000–3,000):** conflates the *warrior* count (Army's early reports of 2,500–3,000 warriors; Michno's revisionist "one thousand" warriors is the low outlier) with the partial Crow's-Nest observation, where scouts "could see only the herd of ponies." - **Total-herd best (~15,000):** the National Park Service states the encampment "had a pony herd of some 15,000 animals" (NPS interpretive text, relayed via National Parks Traveler). Most authoritative *total*-herd number located; not peer-reviewed.
- **High bound (~20,000–25,000):** "20,000 ponies … on a wide grassy bench" is journalism (Billings Gazette); "25,000 ponies" derives from the DeRudio/O'Neil eyewitness account relayed by HistoryNet. Neither is publication-grade for a herd census.
- **Ponies-per-lodge derivation:** none found at page level in Gray (1991) or Michno (1997). The scholarly lodge count is ~1,000 lodges / ~7,000 people; a herd count cannot be derived without a ponies-per-lodge coefficient that no consulted scholarly source supplies.

**Verdict:** the encoded low/best and high measure different quantities (CONFIRMED per prior audit). Split into "herd-visible-at-dawn" and "total herd." Best scholarly total-herd figure: NPS ~15,000 (not peer-reviewed). No publication-grade source supports "20,000."

**B4 — GRAY CHRONOLOGY TIMES & SUN-TIME OFFSET**

| claim id | claim as encoded | current tier | upgraded source | verdict | new conf. | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B4 | minute-level valley times from Gray 1991; sun-time→clock offset unconfirmed | secondary reconstruction | Gray 1991 — methodology chapter NOT consulted at page level | UNRESOLVED (offset) / RELAYED (times) | low | encoded framing itself questionable |

Key correction: **Gray's timing baseline is not local sun time — it is Lt. George D. Wallace's watch, which was set to Chicago time** (confirmed via the CBHMA/Heier review and HistoryNet). The project's "sun-time-to-standard-clock offset" framing is therefore itself suspect. Geometric estimate (longitude ~107.3°W vs. the 90th-meridian Chicago standard, plus the late-June equation of time) implies Chicago watch time ran **~65–66 minutes ahead** of Little Bighorn local apparent solar time — **but this is a derived estimate, NOT read from Gray's page.** A related analyst (Wagner) used a **57-minute** difference, but that was explicitly for **Terry's St. Paul headquarters time**, not Chicago and not Gray. The valley-fight sequence (Reno's charge ~3:03 pm, skirmish/timber ~3:18–3:33, retreat ~3:53, Reno Hill ~4:10) is relayed from Gray-derived reconstructions; the page range in Gray was not confirmed. **Offset: UNRESOLVED. Times: RELAYED.** Do not encode a "sun-time offset" until the exact figure is read from Gray's methodology chapter.

**B5 — THE "225-YARD" SKIRMISH LINE**

| claim id | claim as encoded | current tier | upgraded source | verdict | new conf. | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B5 | Reno valley skirmish line = 225 yards | WFHN | 1879 Reno Court of Inquiry (Graham-prepared transcript, LOC 2011525466); Fox 1993 | CONFIRMED-WEAK | low-med | figure unsourced and contradicted by spacing derivation |

**No publication-grade source gives "225 yards" for Reno's valley skirmish-line frontage.** The 1879 RCOI transcript (contemporary primary document, consulted directly via the UW-Madison digital transcript) establishes the *manner* of deployment — troopers "five to 10 yards apart," every fourth man a horse-holder (per Moylan/Wallace/DeRudio testimony) — but does not state a 225-yard total frontage. A per-man-spacing derivation contradicts the figure: ~112 men on the line (three companies minus horse-holders) at standard ~5-yard skirmish intervals yields ~500+ yards of frontage — materially longer than 225 yards. The encoded figure is both unsourced to publication-grade work and in tension with a derivation from primary testimony. Best available source: RCOI testimony on deployment manner; Fox 1993 for skirmish-line geometry generally. **CONFIRMED-WEAK.**

**B6 — VILLAGE DIMENSIONS & CIRCLE POSITIONS**

| claim id | claim as encoded | current tier | upgraded source | verdict | new conf. | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B6 | village dimensions & circle positions, Michno pp. 17–18 | Michno pp. 17-18 | Michno 1997, pp. 17–19 (relayed via detailed review); Scott 2010 (archaeology) | UPGRADED-MODIFIED / RELAYED | med | no independent archaeological constraint |

The prior hand-check is corroborated at page level through a detailed review quoting Michno directly: using Wooden Leg's description, the camp "hugged the river and was entirely east of the I-90 (Michno, p. 17)"; comparing Moylan's "200-300 yards wide" description, "1,900 lodges would have fit within the space of a quarter square mile (Michno, p. 18)"; the spatial analysis spans pp. 3–20. Michno's overall thesis (relayed via HistoryNet's book review): the village was **~1.5 miles long × ~0.25 mile wide, ~1,200 lodges, ~1,500 warriors** — smaller than tradition holds. Because these pages were reached via reviews, the Michno-specific numbers are **RELAYED.** Independent archaeological constraint on circle positions: **none published.** Scott 2010 (publication-grade, consulted directly) confirms the valley village floor has NOT been systematically surveyed by NPS (it is private / Crow-allotment land); Pitsch worked "portions of two Indian camp areas" privately. Circle positions therefore rest on testimony (Michno; Red Horse's ordering — "the lodges of the Uncpapas were pitched highest up the river") with no independent survey to constrain them.

### GROUP C — THE ARCHAEOLOGICAL GAP

| item | verdict | notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pitsch survey publication status | RELAYED / partially published | valley skirmish-line cartridge map does not exist in publication-grade form |

From publication-grade sources consulted directly (Scott 2010 Technical Report 124; Scott 2010 Oxbow report):
- **Confirmed:** "thousands more artifacts were collected by Jason Pitsch in his study of the Reno Valley fight and portions of two Indian camp areas" on his own private land (Scott 2010, relayed via the ACWRT summary quoting the report verbatim).
- **Partial publication:** Scott 2010 Technical Report 124 includes **Figure 26, "Finds made by Jason Pitsch and mapped in 1993, plotted on the modern aerial photograph"** — a publication-grade rendering of *some* Pitsch find-spots, but a scatter of plotted finds, **NOT a firing-pin-matched cartridge-distribution map of the valley skirmish line** with the analytical treatment the NPS applied to park land.
- **Geomorphology only:** "Pitsch and Keith Wert (2007)" is cited in Scott's Oxbow report for river-channel/oxbow changes — confirmed NOT a cartridge map.
- **Holder/repository:** the bulk of Pitsch's collection remains **privately held on his own land**; individual certified artifacts have entered the market (Skinner Auctioneers lot 1109, "from the private property of Jason Pitsch"). No public-repository or university deposit was located. The 1993 plotted data reached the NPS (it is in Scott's GIS, Figs. 21/26), so find-location data exists in NPS hands in some form, but no published skirmish-line distribution map has been produced.

**Verdict:** the valley skirmish-line cartridge map does NOT exist in publication-grade form. Partial Pitsch find-spots are published (Scott 2010, Fig. 26). The full collection is privately held; access is possible only in principle by direct arrangement with Pitsch or via the NPS Midwest Archeological Center GIS. **This remains the single largest evidentiary gap.**
